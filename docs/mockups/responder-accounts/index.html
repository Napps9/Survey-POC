Notes

  1. 1

    Nothing here is rendered server-side. /play/:token is served from cached_play_page — one HTML body shared by every respondent, keyed on token, updated_at, locale and wave, with the header comment "nothing per-respondent is rendered into it" (player_controller.rb:1189). Adding the session to that key turns a per-link cache into a per-person one on what the same file calls ~45% of a journey's CPU. So the copy is ERB and the state is drawn client-side, exactly as _renderTokenScore and _renderLeaderboard already are.§6

  2. 2

    Recognising a signed-in respondent needs one small JSON fetch — and every same-origin GET a /play/ page makes still goes through the service worker's networkFirst(PAGE_CACHE) handler regardless of path. Excluding that endpoint is a change to the worker's own behaviour, so it is the one thing here that needs a CACHE_VERSION bump.§6

  3. 3

    Vertos legitimately run in third-party iframes (after_action :allow_embedding), where storage is partitioned and the player_session_id cookie is third-party and mostly blocked. The block must degrade rather than look broken — and it must not claim to have found Vertos it cannot see.§2

A sixth state was drawn and cut: a "we already have this address" branch. There isn't one — an address that already has an account simply gets the sign-in link, which is also the only answer that doesn't tell a stranger whether an address is registered.

Notes

  1. 1

    Outside /play/, deliberately. That path is the service worker's whole scope and its HTML is cached for offline use; a signed-in page has no business in a shared device's cache. you joins NOINDEX_PATHS (application_controller.rb:19) and carries Cache-Control: no-store.§6

  2. 2

    The app's own ambient treatment — the four drifting orbs at blur(120px), damped as they are on results surfaces. This is the dashboard's language, not a new one, because a respondent arriving here has already seen the player's.

  3. 3

    Three, not four — and the header says three. Priya played four Vertos on this device; Library opening hours kept no durable identity, so there is nothing to claim it by and the account does not know it exists.   4. 4

    Token piles read from responses.token_totals with that Verto's own token_types — its icons, its names, its amounts. Nothing here is normalised across Vertos, because nothing about them is comparable.§7

  5. 5

    One badge per card, and it is the state that matters: impact awaited, impact delivered, or a follow-up available. A card with none of those is just a card.§5

  6. 6

    Two organisations in one list is the whole point of an account — and it is also the thing the app has always refused to do. The crest, not just the name, so it reads as "who asked me", which is what the mail preferences are then scoped by.§8 §9

The empty state, not drawn: an account with one claimed Verto and nothing else says so plainly and offers nothing to browse — there is no public catalogue of Vertos to send someone to, and inventing one here would be inventing a feature.

Notes

  1. 1

    The wallet is a rail here and a tab on mobile — same numbers, same words. "412 across 3 Vertos and 2 organisations" is a total, and the sentence under it says so; the app already sums across token types inside a Verto (leaderboard_rank_by: "all", LeaderboardStanding#total), so this is that same sum one level up. What does not follow is a board ranking players across Vertos — award sizes are a creator's free choice, so it would measure whose Verto you happened to play.§7

  2. 2

    One follow-up, with the reason on the card. It is the creator's own pointer at another Verto or the next SurveyWave, not a recommendation the app invented — nothing in this codebase knows enough about a respondent to recommend anything, and it should stay that way.§5

  3. 3

    The anonymous name is different on every Verto, and that is correct. PlayerAlias is one row per (survey, identity digest), written once and never updated — Priya is Clever Axolotl on the two council Vertos and Brave Pangolin on Riverside's. Deliberately absent from the rail: a streak, a level, a badge shelf. The tokens a creator awards are a device for getting through their Verto, not a currency this account is allowed to inflate into a game of its own.

Notes

  1. 1

    These are the rows the player already draws. GET /play/:token/results builds them (aggregate_rows:1246), _renderComparison renders them (player_controller.js:2145), and the strings are already written and translated — player.comparison_title is literally "Your answers vs everyone". The account's contribution is that they survive closing the tab, which today they do not.§7

  2. 2

    The "Your answer" pill and the highlighted bar are the existing .play-compare-mine and .play-bar-label.is-mine. Nothing is restyled for this surface — a respondent should recognise the screen they saw at the end of the Verto.

  3. 3

    These numbers can move, and the deck says so rather than pretending otherwise. apply_token_totals recomputes from the current cards on every save (player_controller.rb:1155), and the aggregates behind the bars are cached in 10-second windows — so a creator editing awards changes what a respondent "collected" in May. Either the claim snapshots the totals at completion, or the wallet is honest that it is a live view. §7 puts the decision in front of whoever builds it instead of burying it.§7

  4. 4

    The impact strip is the same content board F carries, in its waiting state — a respondent who opens a Verto months later should find the answer to "what happened?" on the Verto, not only in an email they may have lost.§5

  5. 5

    Share moves here too, and stays useful for as long as the Verto is open: the same card, the same generated image and the same creator-written headline the share deck specifies. A Verto that has closed drops it rather than sending friends to a dead link.

Not drawn, and deliberately: an "export my answers" button. The respondent-data export exists (RespondentDataExport) but is a creator-facing subject-access tool keyed on session_token or a respondent code — it does not know about accounts, and wiring it is §9's job, not a control to sketch here.

Notes

  1. 1

    412 is the same summation the product already performs — LeaderboardStanding#total adds a Verto's token types together whenever leaderboard_rank_by is "all". This is that sum one level up, and it is the only number on the screen that spans Vertos.§7

  2. 2

    Piles come from responses.token_totals, keyed on (survey_id, token_id) — because token ids are not unique. Survey#duplicate! copies token_types verbatim and sanitize_token_types passes creator-supplied ids straight through (survey.rb:1531), so a duplicated Verto shares ids with its original. Keying on the id alone would silently merge two Vertos' piles.§7

  3. 3

    The standing and the anonymous name come from the Verto's own LeaderboardStanding and PlayerAlias, unchanged — including the fact that the name differs per Verto.§7

  4. 4

    Three rows, because the account holds three Vertos. Priya played a fourth on this device — Library opening hours — and it is not here and not greyed out, because a Verto with no durable identity leaves nothing to claim and the account genuinely does not know about it. The header and the list agree, which is the only way a respondent can trust either.§2

  5. 5

    The sentence at the foot is not a disclaimer — it is the design. Anyone reading this wallet will ask why the numbers cannot be compared, and answering it once in the interface is cheaper than answering it in support.

Cut: streaks, levels, badge shelves, and anything that spends. Creator tokens are a device for getting through that creator's Verto; turning them into a platform currency would make every creator's design choice a lever on someone else's score.

Notes

  1. 1

    The organisation is the sender in the reader's eyes; Playverto is the "via". That is the relationship the address was given for, and it is also what makes the per-organisation unsubscribe legible.

  2. 2

    Sent through the existing Comms:: suite, so batching, open/click tracking, suppression and RFC 8058 one-click unsubscribe come free. But not by widening verified_users: every non-list branch of Comms::AudienceResolver emits user_id:, and email_campaign_recipients.user_id has no foreign key — a Player id in that slot would silently dereference to whatever User holds it.   3. 3

  2. 2

Left out, and why

  • A password, and social sign-in. Two Vertos can be six months apart. A password would be forgotten by the second one, and a Google button is a second identity provider to reason about for an account that holds four survey responses.
  • A cross-Verto leaderboard. Award sizes are a creator's free choice, so it would rank people by whose Verto they happened to play. The total stays; the table does not.
  • Streaks, levels, badges, and anything that spends. Creator tokens are a device for getting through that creator's Verto. Making them a platform currency turns every creator's design choice into a lever on someone else's score.
  • A recommendation feed. Three creator-chosen follow-ups is a list. Anything longer needs ranking, and ranking needs a model of the respondent this app has deliberately never built.
  • Giving creators the list of addresses. They see a count (board K) and can publish an update the platform delivers. That is a different relationship from being handed 214 addresses, and it is the one the consent on board B describes.
  • A digest that batches several organisations into one email. The obvious saving and the wrong one: the unsubscribe would have to become per-row inside one message, and the sender in the reader's eyes would stop being the organisation.
  • Per-language overrides of the ask copy, and a "✦ Suggest" generator for it. The thank-you copy beside it isn't translated today either, and three short fields are not worth a Claude call.
  • An impact score, or any aggregate of impact. It would be a number with nothing behind it.
